## Abstract
This is a general protocol for the maintenance, spore generation, and stock creation for fungal isolates. This protocol assumes that a fungal isolate of interest has been isolated onto a nutrient agar plate.

---

- Yeast extract
- Tryptone
- Agar
- NaCl

**Solutions**
- dH2O

- Tween20

**Glassware**

**Plastic**
- Standard 90 mm diameter x 15 mm depth petri dishes
- Inoculation loops
- P1000 pipette tips (sterile)
- P200 pipette tips (sterile)

- Cryotubes

## Method

**Preparing fungal agar plates**

*N.B. This protocol includes the recipe for "homemade" generic fungal agar, but other pre-made agar mixes are available for fungi e.g., PDA and MEA. Additionally some fungi might have more specific media requirements and the recipe may need to be altered to accommodate this.*

1. In a 500 mL glass bottle, add the following:
  - 8 g (D)-glucose anyhydrous
  - 2 g Yeast extract
  - 0.8 g Tryptone
  - 6 g agar
  - 400 mL dH2O

2. Autoclave the solution with the lid loose

3. After autoclaving, while still molten, pour approximately 30 mL (or enough to achieve a thick plate) of agar solution into petri dishes in a sterilised category 2 laminar flow hood. Thicker plates prevent the agar drying out over the longer incubation times of fungi.

*N.B. A flow hood with a glass front and vertical air flow will protect you from spore inhalation. If the fungi is expected to be pathogenic then more protection will be needed.*

4. Once set, plates can be closed, sealed in a plastic bag, and stored at 4 °C until use.

5. To propagate a fungal isolate, use tweezers sterilised with 70 % (v/v) ethanol to cut a small (1 cm x 1 cm) section of a previous nutrient agar plate colonised by the fungal isolate and place in the centre of a new fungal agar plate.

6. Incubate this plate with the agar side at the bottom at 30 °C (or a temperature optimal for the isolate) for 2-8 weeks, checking regularly for spore formation.

*N.B. Spore formation usually occurs once the fungal isolate has colonised the entire plate and a colour or morphology change has occurred. Spore formation will depend heavily on the isolate and may not be obvious. Microscopy of a scrape of the plate may be necessary to confirm when spore formation has occurred. Microscopy of harvested spores is described in the following steps.*

**Harvesting spores**

1. In a 250 mL glass bottle, combine:
   - 0.9 g NaCl
   - 500 μL Tween20
   - 100 mL dH20
  
2. Autoclave the solution with the lid loose and then allow to cool.

3. In a sterilised laminar flow hood, add 2 mL sterile saline solution to the colonised plate.

4. Using a sterile inoculation loop, gently scrape the surface of the plate to release fungal spores into the saline solution. Avoid dislodging large chunks of the plate or fungal mycelia.

*N.B. The amount of spores and the morphology of the fungal isolate may demand more saline solution per plate. It is inevitable that some mycelia and hyphal fragments will be dislodged but this is not an issue as they will be filtered out in the next steps.*

5. Once all spore forming areas of the plate have been scraped, use the P1000 pipette to recover the solution and transfer as much as possible to a 15 or 50 mL falcon tube. If spores are being harvested from multiple plates, then the harvested spores can be combined in the same tube.

*N.B. If this is the first time working with the fungal isolate, this is a good time to look at 10 μL on a slide under a microscope at 40x and 100x magnification to check the morphology of the cells harvested and confirm they have the expected spore morphology.*

**Filtering spores**

1. Prior to filtering, place the spore solution in the flacon tube horizontally on a shaker at 500 rpm for 1 hour followed by three rounds of vortexing at max speed for 1 minute with 10 second rests in-between. This attempts to dislodge clumps of spores.

2. Cut the gauze into 4 x 4 cm squares and layer pieces on top of each other at different angles until you have created a fine mesh that will trap larger hyphal fragments. More squares may be needed based on the size of the gauze, but this approach is much cheaper than using very expensive MiraCloth.

3. Place the gauze in a glass funnel in a falcon tube of the appropriate volume and then pipette the spore solution over the gauze and allow to pass through into the collection tube.

4. With gloves, squeeze the remaining gauze to release any additional spore solution.

**Counting spores** 

*N.B. There is a useful video explaining the use of a haemocytometer [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MZV4_QkonAE)*

1. Combine 10 μL of filtered spore solution with 90 μL of sterile saline solution in a 1.5 mL Eppendorf tube and mix by pipetting up and down.

2. Prepare the haemocytometer and cover slip by wiping with 70 % (v/v) ethanol and allowing to air dry, then secure the cover slip over the counting grid and press until rings of newton (oil slick pattern) appear at the edges.  

3. Load 10 μL of 1/10 diluted spore solution onto the heamocytometer under neath the cover slip, allow to sit for 2 minutes to settle.

4. View the counting grid at 40x magnification under a light microscope and count the number of spores in 5 of the large squares (i.e., four corners and central square).

5. Calculate the spore concentration with the following equation:
   Average number of spores * (1 / Volume of square) * Dilution factor = Spores per mL

*N.B. Heamocytometers can vary in terms of the grid layout and volume of the counting squares. So check carefully that you know the volume held over the squares you prefer to count.*

6. The counted spore solution can now be used to inoculate liquid cultures with a known concentration of spores, and the harvested plates can still be used for propagation of new fungal plates.

**Preparing a glycerol stock**

1. In a 200 mL glass bottle combine the following:
  - 50 mL glycerol
  - 50 mL dH2O

2. Autoclave with the lid loose and then allow to cool.

3. Combine 1 mL of filtered and counted spore solution with 1 mL of sterile 50 % (v/v) glycerol in a cryotube and mix by pipetting up and down.

4. Store at -70 °C.

## Additional notes
This is a general protocol which might not be appropriate for all fungi but is a good starting point.

## Disclaimer

Fungi release airborne spores and therefore you should take all precautions when working with fungi to avoid inhalation, especially with unknown or uncharacterised isolates.

---

## Warning

Always autoclave bottles with the lids loose and always work with fungi in a flow hood with a glass front and vertical airflow to avoid inhalation or release of spores.
