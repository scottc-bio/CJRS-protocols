## Abstract
Due to the growing interest in microbial degradation of various hydrocarbons, protocols for screening the ability to utilise these carbon sources are required. However, due to the difficult properties of these hydrocarbons it is often not simple to screen microbial growth in liquid cultures. Here for various hydrocarbons, methods for preparing 10 mL solutions of minimal media + hydrocarbons will be described alongside suggestions for growth conditions that will provide accessibility to the hydrocarbon substrates for microbes.

## Method

**Hexadecane**
Hexadecane is insoluble in aqueous solutions and therefore the best approach is to make a stock in ethanol and dilute from this.

1. In a fume hood combine 1 mL of hexadecane with 9 mL absolute ethanol in a 15 mL falcon tube to give a 10 % stock.

2. Transfer the stock to a 10 mL syringe, attach a 25 μm pore filter and filter sterilise in the laminar flow hood into a sterile 15 mL falcon tube.

3. In a sterilised laminar flow hood, add 9.75 mL of minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

4. In a fume hood add 250 μL of the sterilised 10 % (v/v) hexadecane to give a final concentration of 0.25 % (v/v).

**1-Hexadecanol**
Supplied as a waxy solid, insoluble in aqueous solutions. The best approach is to make a stock in ethanol of known concentration then add this to a liquid culture where the precipitant will be distributed through the culture by agitation.

1. Weigh 1.2125 g of solid 1-hexadecanol into a 25 mL beaker

2. Add 8 mL absolute ethanol and stir with a magnetic stirrer with mild warming. It should dissolve quickly.

3. Transfer to a measuring cylinder and make up to 10 mL with ethanol to create the 500 mM stock solution.

4. Transfer the solution to a 10 mL syringe, attach a 25 μm pore filter and filter sterilise in the laminar flow hood into a sterile 15 mL falcon tube.

5. In the laminar flow hood, add 9.8 mL minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

6. Add 200 μL of the 500 mM 1-hexadecanol stock to give a final concentration of 10 mM. The 1-hexadecanol will precipitate.

7. For growth assays, agitation is required with the falcon tube at an angle to distribute the hexadecane throughout the culture.

**Dodecyl aldehyde**
Supplied as a greasy insoluble solid. I tried ethanol and DMSO and neither was perfect but DMSO yielded better results. Always work in the fume hood as it has a strong odor (it is used in the fragrance industry).

1. Weigh 0.922 g in the fume hood of solid dodecyl aldehyde into a 25 mL beaker.

2. Add 8 mL DMSO and stir with a magnetic stirrer with warming. It will not dissolve perfectly but will turn to liquid at least.

3. Transfer to a measuring cylinder and make up to 10 mL with DMSO to create the 500 mM stock solution and store in a 15 mL falcon tube.

4. Transfer the solution to a 10 mL syringe while still warm, attach a 25 μm pore filter and filter sterilise in the laminar flow hood into a sterile 15 mL falcon tube.

5. In the laminar flow hood, add 9.8 mL minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

6. Prior to adding to a growth culture, warm the sterile 500 mM stock in a water bath at 100 °C to re-melt any solidified dodecyl aldehyde.

7. Add 200 μL of the warm 500 mM dodecyl aldehyde to the minimal media to give a final concentration of 10 mM. This should form a translucent solution but with no precipitant.

**Sodium dodecanoate**
Supplied as a white powder and is soluble in water. Has surfactant like properties.

1. Weigh 1.1115 g in the fume hood and add to a 25 mL beaker.

2. Add 8 mL dH2O and stir with a magnetic stirrer with warming.

3. Transfer to a measuring cylinder and make up to 10 mL with dH2O to create the 500 mM stock solution and store in a 15 mL falcon tube.

4. Transfer the solution to a 10 mL syringe, attach a 25 μm pore filter and filter sterilise in the laminar flow hood into a sterile 15 mL falcon tube.

5. Add 200 μL of the warm 500 mM sodium dodecanoate to the minimal media to give a final concentration of 10 mM. This should form a milky white solution.
