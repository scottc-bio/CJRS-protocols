## Abstract
Due to the growing interest in microbial degradation of various hydrocarbons, protocols for screening the ability to utilise these carbon sources are required. However, due to the difficult properties of these hydrocarbons it is often not simple to screen microbial growth in liquid cultures. Here for various hydrocarbons, methods for preparing 10 mL solutions of minimal media + hydrocarbons will be described alongside suggestions for growth conditions that will provide accessibility to the hydrocarbon substrates for microbes.

## Method

**Hexadecane**

Hexadecane is insoluble in aqueous solutions and therefore the best approach is to make a stock in ethanol and dilute from this.

1. In a fume hood combine 1 mL of hexadecane with 9 mL absolute ethanol in a 15 mL falcon tube to give a 10 % stock. The ethanol will keep it sterile.

2. In a sterilised laminar flow hood, add 9.75 mL of minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

3. In a fume hood add 250 μL of the 10 % (v/v) hexadecane to give a final concentration of 0.25 % (v/v).

**1-Hexadecanol**

Supplied as a waxy solid, insoluble in aqueous solutions. The best approach is to make a stock in ethanol of known concentration then add this to a liquid culture where the precipitant will be distributed through the culture by agitation.

1. Weigh 1.2125 g of solid 1-hexadecanol into a 25 mL beaker

2. Add 8 mL absolute ethanol and stir with a magnetic stirrer with mild warming. It should dissolve quickly.

3. Transfer to a measuring cylinder and make up to 10 mL with ethanol to create the 500 mM stock solution.

4. Transfer the solution a sterile 15 mL falcon tube, the ethanol will keep this sterile.

5. In the laminar flow hood, add 9.8 mL minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

6. Add 200 μL of the 500 mM 1-hexadecanol stock to give a final concentration of 10 mM. The 1-hexadecanol will precipitate.

7. For growth assays, agitation is required with the falcon tube at an angle to distribute the hexadecane throughout the culture.

**Dodecyl aldehyde**

Supplied as a greasy insoluble solid. I tried ethanol and DMSO and neither was perfect but DMSO yielded better results. Always work in the fume hood as it has a strong odor (it is used in the fragrance industry).

1. Weigh 0.922 g in the fume hood of solid dodecyl aldehyde into a 25 mL beaker.

2. Add 8 mL DMSO and stir with a magnetic stirrer with warming. It will not dissolve perfectly but will turn to liquid at least.

3. Transfer to a measuring cylinder and make up to 10 mL with DMSO to create the 500 mM stock solution and store in a 15 mL falcon tube.

4. Transfer the solution to a 10 mL syringe while still warm and dissolved, attach a 25 μm pore filter and filter sterilise in the fume hood into a sterile 15 mL falcon tube. A fume hood is not truly sterile, but it is safer to work in here.

5. In a laminar flow hood, add 9.8 mL minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

6. Prior to adding to a growth culture, warm the sterile 500 mM stock in a water bath at 100 °C to re-melt any solidified dodecyl aldehyde.

7. Add 200 μL of the warm 500 mM dodecyl aldehyde to the minimal media to give a final concentration of 10 mM. This will form a white precipitant that needs to be distributed with agitation.

**Sodium dodecanoate**

Supplied as a white powder and is soluble in water. Has surfactant like properties.

1. Weigh 1.1115 g in the fume hood and add to a 25 mL beaker.

2. Add 8 mL dH2O and stir with a magnetic stirrer with warming.

3. Transfer to a measuring cylinder and make up to 10 mL with dH2O to create the 500 mM stock solution and store in a 15 mL falcon tube.

4. Transfer the solution to a 10 mL syringe, attach a 25 μm pore filter and filter sterilise in the laminar flow hood into a sterile 15 mL falcon tube.

5. Add 200 μL of the warm 500 mM sodium dodecanoate to the minimal media to give a final concentration of 10 mM. This should form a milky white solution.

*N.B. After leaving at room temperature, this solution will solidify but can be re-melted into the liquid stock by placing in a water bath at 100 °C for a few minutes.*

**Isooctane**

Supplied as flammable and volatile liquid. Always handle the neat bottle in the fume hood and keep stored in a cabinet for flammables.

1. In the fume hood, combine 1 mL isooctane with 9 mL absolute ethanol in a 25 mL glass bottle to create the 10 % (v/v) isooctane stock.

2. In the laminar flow hood, add 9.75 mL minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

3. Add 250 μL of the 10 % (v/v) stock to give a final concentration of 0.25 %. No precipitant should form.

**Ethylbenzene**

Ethylbenzene is toxic if inhaled. Only handle the neat liquid, any diluted stocks, or any cultures containing ethylbenzene in the fume hood. Always wear safety glasses, and a mask. Store all stock and diluted solutions in an appropriate fume hood cabinet.

1. In the fume hood, combine 1 mL ethylbenzene with 9 mL absolute ethanol in a 25 mL glass bottle to create the 10 % (v/v) ethylbenzene stock.

2. In the laminar flow hood, add 9.75 mL minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

3. In the fume hood, add 250 μL of the 10 % (v/v) stock to give a final concentration of 0.25 %. No precipitant should form.

**Phenylacetic acid**

Has a strong musty odour that will linger for a while, but is only classified as an eye irritant. Wear a mask for the smell and handle in the fume-hood as much as possible.

1. In the fume hood weigh out 0.68075 g of phenylacetic acid and add to a 25 mL glass beaker.

2. Add 8 mL DMSO and swirl to dissolve.

3. Transfer to a 25 mL measuring cylinder and make up to 10 mL with DMSO to create the 500 mM phenylacetic acid stock solution.

4. In the laminar flow-hood, transfer this solution to a 10 mL syringe and attach a 25 μm pore size filter and filter sterilise the solution into a sterile 15 mL falcon tube.

5. In the laminar flow hood, add 9.8 mL minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

6. Add 200 μL of the 500 mM phenylacetic acid to the minimal media to give a final concentration of 10 mM.

**Catechol (1,2-dihydroxybenzene)**

Very toxic if ingested, inhaled, or if it comes into contact with skin. Take all necessary precautions of fume hood, mask, gloves, and labcoat.

1. In the fume hood weigh out 0.55056 g of catechol in a 25 mL glass beaker.

2. Add 8 mL dH2O and stir with a magnetic stirrer to dissovle.

3. Transfer to a 25 mL measuring cylinder and make up to 10 mL with dH2O to create the 500 mM catechol stock solution.

4. Transfer to a 25 mL glass bottle.

5. In the laminar flow hood, add 9.8 mL minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

5. In the fume-hood, add 200 μL of the 500 mM catechol to the minimal media to give a final concentration of 10 mM.

*N.B. Although not truly sterile, I believe it is safer to always work in the fume hood for catechol. I would avoid filter sterilisation which can be messy as you want to avoid distributing catechol around.*

**Sodium benzoate**

Supplied as a white powder, used as a food additive so not a huge risk associated with its use.

1. Weigh out 0.720525 g of sodium benzoate into a 25 mL glass beaker.

2. Add 8 mL dH2O and stir with a magnetic stirrer to dissolve.

3. Transfer to a 25 mL measuring cylinder and make up to 10 mL with dH2O to create the 500 mM sodium benzoate stock.

4. In the laminar flow-hood, transfer this solution to a 10 mL syringe and attach a 25 μm pore size filter and filter sterilise the solution into a sterile 15 mL falcon tube.

5. In the laminar flow hood, add 9.8 mL minimal media to a 50 mL falcon tube.

6. Add 200 μL of the 500 mM sodium benzoate to the minimal media to give a final concentration of 10 mM.

## Additional notes

Use whichever size glass vessels are appropriate and feel free to change volumes and concentrations for your needs. This is not optimised for microbial growth.

## Disclaimer

Some of these carbon sources are toxic and I suggest taking all necessary precautions for all substrates to be as safe as possible. This includes using a fume hood, always wearing a lab coat, mask, gloves, and glasses.

---

## Guidelines

Make sure you have understood the risks of all carbon sources prior to opening any. Read the SDS reports for all chemicals to understand the risks.

---

## Warning

Inhalation and eye contact with any of these substrates poses a risk. So I suggest always working in the fume hood where possible. For the more toxic substrates, ensure your safety with lab coats, masks, glasses, and gloves. Any plastics or glasses that come in to contact with toxic substrates should be rinsed using the sinks in the fume hoods and be left to evaporate.

Disposal of any waste will depend on your lab and local regulations. Don't throw any of these substrates in general waste or down the sink.
